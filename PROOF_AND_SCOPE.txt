EXPONENT-FOUR CAYLEY GRAPHS: A COMPLETE CLASSIFICATION CANDIDATE
Prepared 2026-10-10. This is an independently checkable proof, not an assertion
of established publication priority or peer review.

Target: Krebs and Sankar, Abelian groups without 3-chromatic Cayley graphs,
arXiv:2410.11028v2 (23 February 2026), Section 5.2, Question 5.3.
https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11028v2
Their question asks which nonabelian exponent-four groups admit a Cayley graph
of chromatic number three. It does not require this graph to be connected.

THEOREM. Let G be any nonabelian group of exponent four. Then G admits an
undirected Cayley graph of chromatic number three if and only if G is not
isomorphic to Q8 x E, where E is an elementary abelian two-group.
Here E may be infinite. We work in the usual set theory with choice, as does
the infinite-group portion of the source paper.

Equivalently, the positive cases are exactly the groups having a section
isomorphic to the dihedral group D8 of order eight. A section means a quotient
H/N of a subgroup H of G by a normal subgroup N of H.

All Cayley graphs below are simple: their inverse-closed connection sets omit
the identity. They can be disconnected, which is explicitly permitted by the
target question.

1. GROUP-THEORETIC LEMMA

We prove that a nonabelian exponent-four group with no D8 section is Q8 x E.
No finite-group classification or local-finiteness theorem is needed.

First, any two involutions commute. If involutions a,b did not commute, then
ab would have order four, and <a,b> would be D8: a and b are reflections and
their product is a rotation of order four. Thus the set T of elements whose
square is the identity is a normal elementary abelian subgroup (including the
identity in T).

In fact every involution is central. Suppose t is an involution and x does not
commute with t. Since all involutions commute with t, x has order four.
Put t'=x t x^-1 and A=<t,t'>. Conjugation by x on T has order at most two,
because x^2 belongs to T and T is abelian. Hence x interchanges t and t'.
As t' differs from t, A is a Klein four-group, normal in H=<A,x>.
The square w=x^2 is fixed by conjugation by x. If w lies in A, the only
nonidentity fixed element of A is tt', so w=tt'. Then H has order eight and
is a nonabelian group with a noncentral involution, hence D8. More explicitly,
t x t=x^-1 follows from [t,x]=tt'=x^2.
If w is outside A, H has order sixteen, <w> is central, and H/<w> has order
eight and is the nontrivial semidirect product A semidirect C2. It is D8.
Both alternatives contradict the assumed absence of a D8 section.

Every square in G is now central. The subgroup generated by all squares is
central, and its quotient has exponent two and is therefore abelian. Thus G
has nilpotence class at most two. For x,y in G, [x,y]^2=[x^2,y]=1, so every
commutator is a central involution.

For noncommuting x,y, put a=x^2, b=y^2, c=[x,y], using additive vector-space
notation for central involutions when convenient. We claim a=b=c. The central
element c is nonzero. If a differs from c, there is a linear functional ell
on C=<a,b,c> with ell(c)=1 and ell(a)=0. If a=c but b differs from c, choose
ell(c)=1 and ell(b)=0 instead. Quotient H=<x,y> by ker ell. Since H/C has
order four, H/(ker ell) has order eight. Its commutator is nontrivial, and
at least one of its two generating elements is an involution. This group is
D8, again a forbidden section. Therefore a=b=c. In particular every
noncommuting pair generates Q8.

Choose one quaternion subgroup K=<i,j>, with i^2=j^2=[i,j]=z. For any g in G,
the commutator [i,g] is either 1 or z: if i and g fail to commute, the preceding
paragraph gives [i,g]=i^2=z. The same holds for j. Thus g normalizes K and
acts on i and j by independent changes of sign. All four such automorphisms
are inner automorphisms of K. Consequently G=K C_G(K).

The centralizer C_G(K) has exponent two. Indeed, for h in that centralizer,
the elements ih and j do not commute, with commutator z. The established
noncommuting-pair identity gives (ih)^2=j^2=z. Since h commutes with i,
(ih)^2=i^2 h^2=z h^2; therefore h^2=1. Hence C_G(K) is elementary abelian and
central, and K intersect C_G(K)=<z>. Choose a vector-space complement E to
<z> in C_G(K). Then G=K x E, as claimed.

Conversely, Q8 x E has no D8 section. One can either establish this directly
from its central involutions and shared quaternion square, or use the graph
arguments below: a D8 section would yield a three-chromatic Cayley graph,
whereas every Cayley graph on Q8 x E is cubelike and cannot be three-chromatic.
The latter establishes the converse without an extra group-classification
claim.

2. A D8 SECTION GIVES A THREE-CHROMATIC CAYLEY GRAPH

Write D8=<r,s | r^4=s^2=1, srs=r^-1>. Take
S0={r^2,s,rs}.
All three elements are involutions. The graph Cay(D8,S0) is cubic and has
the odd cycle
1, r^2, r^2 s, r, r s, 1.
An explicit proper three-coloring is given below, with vertices r^a s^b
ordered by a+4b:
  a+4b: 0 1 2 3 4 5 6 7
  color: 0 0 1 2 1 1 2 0.
Thus its chromatic number is exactly three. The independent script also
checks its multiplication table, edges, coloring, and nonbipartiteness.

If H/N is a D8 section of G, let pi:H -> D8 be the quotient map and put
S=pi^-1(S0). This is inverse-closed and excludes the identity. Cay(H,S) is
the independent-fiber blowup of Cay(D8,S0): each fiber of pi is independent,
and two fibers are completely joined exactly when their quotient vertices
are adjacent. Pulling back the displayed coloring proves three-colorability.
Choosing one representative per quotient vertex exhibits a copy of the
quotient graph and proves nonbipartiteness. Finally Cay(G,S) is a disjoint
union of copies of Cay(H,S), on the left cosets of H. Its chromatic number
is exactly three. This proof is also valid for an infinite kernel or group.

3. EVERY CAYLEY GRAPH ON Q8 x E IS CUBELIKE

Let z=-1 in Q8, and write each quaternion uniquely as z^epsilon t(u), where
epsilon is in F2, u is in F2^2, and the fixed transversal is
t(00)=1, t(10)=i, t(01)=j, t(11)=k.
Take the vertex bijection
  (z^epsilon t(u),e) -> (u,epsilon,e)
from Q8 x E to the elementary abelian group F2^3 x E.

If an inverse-closed connection set S contains (q,e) with q noncentral,
then it contains both (q,e) and (zq,e), since q^-1=zq and e^-1=e. Therefore
membership in S for a noncentral quaternion coset is independent of its sign.

For vertices x=(z^epsilon t(u),e) and y=(z^eta t(v),f), the quaternion part
of x^-1 y has quotient coordinate u+v. If u differs from v, its sign may
include a quaternion multiplication cocycle, but membership in S ignores
that sign because both variants are present. If u=v, the quaternion part
is exactly z^(epsilon+eta), with no cocycle. In both cases adjacency is
equivalent to the vector difference (u+v,epsilon+eta,e+f) lying in the image
T of S under the vertex bijection. Hence
  Cay(Q8 x E,S) is isomorphic to Cay(F2^3 x E,T).

Payan's theorem forbids chromatic number three for finite cubelike graphs.
For infinite E, this remains true either by the abelian exponent-two result
of Krebs and Sankar, or by a finite reduction: an odd cycle uses finitely
many E coordinates, which generate a finite subgroup E0; a proper global
three-coloring would restrict to the finite Cayley graph on Q8 x E0 with
connection set S intersect (Q8 x E0), producing a nonbipartite three-colorable
finite cubelike graph, a contradiction.

This proves the negative direction and completes the classification.

4. VERIFICATION AND STATUS

The file verify_small.py uses only Python's standard library. It checks
associativity and inverses in an explicit quaternion multiplication table,
checks the cubelike coordinate isomorphism on every inverse orbit for
Q8 x C2^r with r=0,1,2,3 (orders 8,16,32,64), and exhaustively examines all
inverse-closed connection sets for r=0,1. The 16 Q8 sets give 8 bipartite and
8 non-three-colorable graphs. The 512 order-sixteen sets give 113 bipartite
and 399 non-three-colorable graphs. None has chromatic number three. These
finite computations support the explicit proof; they do not replace it.
